🏹 What is Teer?

Teer is Meghalaya's traditional archery-based lottery, run by the state government under the Meghalaya Amusement and Betting Tax Act. The game has deep cultural roots — archery is a traditional Khasi sport, and Teer turns it into a regulated betting game.

Here's how it works: a group of archers (usually 30–50) shoot arrows at a bamboo target over several rounds. Officials count the arrows that hit the target. The last two digits of that count become the winning number (00–99). For example, if 1,247 arrows hit, the result is 47. If 1,500 hit, the result is 00.

Each Teer game has two rounds daily: the First Round (FR) and Second Round (SR), each with its own winning number. Players can bet on either or both rounds. The most common bets are:

  • Direct (single number): pick the exact 2-digit winning number — pays the highest.
  • House/Ending: pick just the first or last digit — lower payout, better odds.
  • Forecast: predict both rounds' numbers correctly — the biggest payout.

Teer is legal only in Meghalaya, run Monday to Saturday. There are no Teer draws on Sunday.
